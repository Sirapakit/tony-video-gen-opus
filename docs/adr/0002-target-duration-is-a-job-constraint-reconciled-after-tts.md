# Target duration is a Job constraint, reconciled against real narration audio

Actual duration is only known after the narration audio exists, so Target duration is not derived from the Story alone. The Story generator budgets per Scene from the Target duration; real audio is then generated and the total is checked against the Target with a tolerance. If it is out of tolerance, first adjust Voice speed within a natural-sounding range, then send only the offending Scenes back to the LLM to shorten or extend, and show every adjustment at the review step.

Evidence so far (one local prototype run, OmniVoice on Apple Silicon, 3 Thai lines, 4 seeds; small samples, not a ranking of methods): with a fixed seed, `speed` 0.9 and 1.1 changed a line's duration by a median 1.107x and 0.916x relative to the same seed at speed 1.0 (ideal 1.111x and 0.909x; ranges 0.989-1.143 and 0.874-0.954), and the same seed reproduced identical audio in the same process. Separately, the model's fixed-duration argument missed the length it was asked for by a median 3.5 percent (max 8.2, 18 calls). These are different metrics on different samples and are not compared. Two consequences: the characters-per-second rate used for the Story budget varied from about 7.6 to 10.3 across two runs of two models, and the model order flipped between runs, so no rate can be assumed for a provider or model; it is calibrated for each combination of provider, model and saved Voice (and seed) and re-measured when any of them changes. And any duration adjustment is still re-synthesised and re-measured, never predicted. Tolerance is a product policy set independently of any of these numbers.
